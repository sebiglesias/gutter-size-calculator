Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference in capacity between 5-inch and 6-inch gutters?
A 6-inch K-style gutter holds roughly 40% to 50% more water volume than a standard 5-inch gutter. If your roof has steep slopes (6/12 pitch or greater), long runs, or metal panels where water sheds rapidly, 6-inch gutters prevent rainwater from shooting over the outer lip during severe storms.
How many downspouts are required per foot of gutter?
Standard architectural practice (SMACNA) dictates that one downspout should be installed for every 30 to 40 linear feet of gutter run. If using oversized 3x4 inch downspouts rather than small 2x3 inch pipes, drainage velocity doubles, preventing clogging from leaves and shingle grit.
What slope should gutters have for proper gravity drainage?
Gutters should slope downward toward the downspout at a minimum gradient of 1/4 inch per 10 feet of horizontal run (or 1/2 inch per 10 feet for maximum debris self-flushing). A completely flat gutter will collect standing water, breeding mosquitoes and rusting seams.
